## B. VO version (spoken text, numbers spelled out)

**S1 cold open:** On November eleventh, Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation. Effective immediately. He co-founded the company in nineteen ninety-three, and ran it for thirty-three years. The filing gives no reason. This is what is confirmed. What has been reported. And what is only alleged.

**S2 Megaspeed:** The story runs through Megaspeed. A Singapore-based cloud company, and an Nvidia customer. According to The New York Times, Megaspeed's Malaysian affiliate has spent four point eight billion dollars on A.I. chips since June twenty twenty-four. U.S. officials have been investigating whether some of that hardware ended up in China. Singapore police are investigating the company, for suspected breaches of local law. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. It says it takes export rules "extremely seriously," and will cooperate with U.S. authorities.

**S3 report:** On October twenty-ninth, the Times reported that U.S. intelligence officials believe Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive, and no relation, is a Chinese intelligence asset. The Times also reported she was close to Jensen Huang. An Nvidia spokesperson said his interactions with her were "not unusual or unexpected," and that meetings with customers are part of a C-E-O's job. China's foreign ministry called the report "unsubstantiated." And "nothing but smears against China."

**S4 admission:** Ten days later, on November eighth, Huang emailed Nvidia's employees. "I was unfaithful to my wife," he wrote. He named no one. But he addressed "the national security concerns raised in news reports." And said he would cooperate fully. The same day, Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation. It said it had no reason to believe national security, or Nvidia secrets, were compromised. A company spokesperson said Huang was committed to continuing as C-E-O, without interruption. Two days later, the board's independent directors hired the law firm Wachtell Lipton to investigate.

**S5 fall:** The Times reported that the Justice Department had taken over a federal probe of Megaspeed. Then, on November eleventh, three days after that pledge, the board accepted his resignation. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim C-E-O. Longtime director Harvey Jones became Nvidia's first-ever board chair. On November twelfth, Nvidia's shares fell as much as fourteen percent, before closing down about four percent. A day later, its market value closed below four trillion dollars, for the first time since July twenty twenty-five.

**S6 Washington:** On November fourth, the Republican chairs of three House committees had asked Huang for a closed-door briefing. House committees want a closed-door interview. Huang reportedly agreed. The date is still being negotiated. Senators from both parties are reportedly pressing too.

**S7 close:** None of the espionage allegations has been proven. Here's what to watch. Nvidia's earnings on November seventeenth. The board's investigation. And Huang's testimony.
